## Purpose / Big Picture

The pad wires are routed bundle by bundle (`translation/electrical/river_routing.py`): row i of a bundle takes corridor lane c_i on the exit side and channel height h_i by its row index, nested so that the wires of one bundle never cross. Two bundles whose channel runs overlap in x get the same heights and collide; on `cascade_16_folded` the lone heater of column A runs under column B's exit to its pad and blocks column B's wires, although three distinct heights would carry all three (the owner's observation on the render, 2026-09-29). The general rule is channel routing: every pad wire is one horizontal run between its corridor lane and its pad column; the runs of all bundles are assigned tracks jointly under the vertical constraints (at its corridor column a wire lies below every run crossing that column, at its pad column above), with the constrained left-edge algorithm. The per-bundle nesting and the inward nesting become consequences of the constraints, not rules. After this plan `river_routing.py` is that channel router, the six cascades verify clean, and the pad-choice rules are the next item (owner: pads are not yet chosen to minimize the routing; that comes after).

## Surprises & Discoveries

- 2026-10-05: with the channel sized from the track count, `cascade_16_folded` routes every wire through the channel router. The "one-sided pad-row capacity limit" of the eight-row-bundles plan (eleven pads for nine slots at the 130 um minimum pitch) was not a capacity limit; it was the per-bundle nesting this plan removed. The owner's open decision there (two-sided pads, a wider fold, the diagonal) is moot.
- 2026-10-05: the global track count exceeds the widest bundle whenever a lone heater's corridor lane lies within a pitch of a neighbouring bundle's outermost pad column (`cascade_12_folded`: `heater_extra_0`'s lane at cell 681 against `heater_post_5`'s pad column at 683, pitch 3 cells): the lone heater must sit below all six nested rows, seven tracks for a channel of six. The headroom check did not catch it because track 1 still cleared the terminal rows; the wire (`heater_extra_9:l`, forced to the lowest track by its own inward-pushed block) collided with an obstacle inside the layout instead, ten um below the layout's top edge.
- 2026-10-05: the per-wire fallback is unreached on every fixture and benchmark now; its two e2e tests take one wire away from the channel router's result instead of relying on a benchmark that happens to need it.


## Decision Log

- 2026-09-29 (owner): fix the routing generally, not with special cases; the pad-choice rules are the next item after that.
- 2026-10-05 (lead): the channel height is the router's track count exactly, not the larger of that and the widest bundle: a bundle whose rows do not all nest (two rows on one track) needs fewer tracks than rows, and a widest-bundle floor would only pad the channel; intervals without a known exit side keep their item count as a floor since they get no lanes. The track count is computed in the pad plan on the same inputs the router builds (shared helpers), so the headroom check in the router stays a safety net only.
- Open (owner): the commit of the working tree. Declined on 2026-09-29 while the regression was open; the regression is closed.
- 2026-09-29 (lead): the formulation. Wire W: corridor lane x c_W (as today, per bundle: exit column plus i times the track pitch outward), pad column p_W, span [min(c_W, p_W), max(c_W, p_W)], path port -> (c_W, row) -> (c_W, t_W) -> (p_W, t_W) -> pad. Vertical constraints: for every other wire R whose span contains c_W, R lies above W; for every R whose span contains p_W, W lies above R (a straight L, p_W = c_W, therefore tolerates no crossing run at all; if one exists the constraints form a cycle and the wire goes to the per-wire fallback, which is where the pad choice of the next item comes in). Tracks from the bottom (farthest from the pads) with the constrained left-edge algorithm: wires sorted by left edge; a wire joins the current track when it clears the track's last wire by one pitch in x and every wire that must lie below it is already placed; a round without progress means a cycle: those wires fall back. Track t at pad access edge minus (T + 1 - t) pitches, T the number of tracks used, which must fit above the highest terminal row plus clearance. Every placed wire is then checked against obstacles, other terminals and other pads as today and committed; the fallback search runs last for the rest.


## Outcomes & Retrospective

The pad wires are channel-routed: one joint track assignment over every bundle (`river_routing.assign_tracks`, pure, unit-tested on synthetic wire sets), corridor lanes per bundle as before, the per-bundle nesting and the inward nesting gone as rules (`PadAssignment.nesting` deleted) and present only as consequences of the two crossing constraints. The pad plan sizes the channel from the same track assignment through shared helpers. All six cascades verify clean, every pad wire of every cascade channel-routes, the four suite cases are byte-identical to their baseline, `heater_s_mod` keeps detour 0 on every wire. What the first slice got wrong and the second fixed: sizing the channel from the widest bundle (the global count is larger whenever a lone wire must sit under a nested bundle) and packing from the bottom (an unconstrained wire sank to the layout's edge). Lesson: the pad plan and the router must compute the same lanes and pad columns through the same functions, or the plan's sizing and the router's need drift apart silently; the headroom check is a safety net, not the sizing. Next (owner's queue): the pad-choice rules (pads chosen to minimize the routing, the first item after this plan), the compact diagonal, per-waveguide length labels, the other dump circuits.
