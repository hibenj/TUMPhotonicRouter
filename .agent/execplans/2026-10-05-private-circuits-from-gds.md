# The owner's tape-out circuits as benchmarks: extracted from GDS, rebuilt with the open-source PDK, routed

This ExecPlan is a living document. The sections `Progress`, `Surprises & Discoveries`, `Decision Log`, and `Outcomes & Retrospective` must be kept up to date as work proceeds. This document must be maintained in accordance with `.agent/PLANS.md`.

Status: **2026-10-05, written from a measured prototype; Milestone 1 starting. Photonic routing only (owner); the circuits and everything derived from them stay out of git.**


## Purpose / Big Picture

The owner's project dump holds five tape-out circuits as GDS files (confidential: read in place, never copied into the repository or anywhere else; the files are named here only by the owner's own file names). They are the real test cases for the router beyond the cascades: two Clements meshes, a beam-splitter circuit, a sender-and-receiver circuit and a ring circuit, from three tape-outs on two foundry PDKs. No netlists exist, and the foundry component cells cannot be used (the repository routes with the open-source generic PDK of gdsfactory). After this plan a tool extracts each circuit's components, placements and nets from its GDS, rebuilds the circuit with generic PDK components placed so that their ports lie exactly where the original ports lie, writes a benchmark module into the git-ignored package `benchmarks/private/`, and the flow routes it: `python -m photonic_router route private.<name>` ends with a `SUMMARY` line and writes the routed GDS to `build/`. Electrical routing is off for these (the owner: the GDS files carry no electrical routing to compare against; the tool records bond pads but the benchmarks do not use them).

## Surprises & Discoveries

- 2026-10-05: the files are not flat. Every one is a deep hierarchy (4 to 7 levels) of IPKISS-style cells with descriptive names: foundry component cells (an MMI2x2, a thermo-optic phase shifter, grating couplers of several kinds, edge couplers, bond pads), sub-circuit containers, and one leaf cell per waveguide whose name encodes its two ends (`<source>_<port>_to_<target>_<port>`, truncated at the GDS name limit). Components and placements therefore come from the hierarchy, not from geometry recognition; only the connectivity needs geometry, because the names are truncated and the port vocabularies differ between tape-outs.
- 2026-10-05: connectivity by merging works. All waveguide polygons of a circuit on its core layer, merged into one region, give one polygon per net (chains of waveguide pieces merge by themselves; the pieces overlap rather than butt, which defeated an edge-matching first attempt); a net's ports are the polygon's short edges (one waveguide width long) that lie on a component's boundary. On the beam-splitter circuit this gives 57 nets with exactly two ports and 4 with one (its two-port polarization-splitting couplers, whose ports lie inside the cell box); the MMI ports come out at the cell-local positions (0, +-1.0) and (65, +-1.0), the heater ports at (0, 0) and (146, 0), every 1D grating coupler port at (421, 0).
- 2026-10-05: three things the boundary test misses, each with a general remedy: (1) couplers whose port lies inside the cell box (the island grating couplers, the 2D couplers): the port is the short edge adjacent to the component's own shapes, so the test must be "touches the component's shapes", not "lies on its box"; (2) placeholder cells with no geometry at all (the edge couplers and end caps of the first Clements mesh, 24 and 42 cells): a net end within a few um of such a cell's origin is that cell's port, and a net ending at an end cap is a terminated stub, not a route; (3) a net may end on the second waveguide layer: in the sender-and-receiver circuit the core layer stops 3 um short of each heater while the second layer (a transition section) reaches it, so the merge must take the union of a net cell's waveguide layers.
- 2026-10-05: the second Clements file carries about 700 unconnected MMI "show" cells of other chips of the same tape-out (12 name variants times 54) beside its 33 real ones; a component with no net on any port is dropped. Its classification also needs grating couplers recognized before MMIs (`..._MMIBs_fgc_a_in_0` is a coupler of the "MMI beam splitter" scheme).
- 2026-10-05: the ring circuit's ring is a container cell (ring, two coupling waveguides, two bus waveguides, two joining nets) and its heaters are `*_heater` cells that carry the waveguide and the metal; both need the configuration's composite-component rule rather than the leaf rule.
- 2026-10-05: the generic components can reproduce the original port geometry exactly, which the prototype of the cascades never needed: `mmi2x2(length_mmi, length_taper, gap_mmi, width_taper)` puts its ports at `x = -length_taper` and `x = length_mmi + length_taper`, `y = +-(gap_mmi + width_taper) / 2`, so an original MMI with ports 65 um apart at +-1.0 um is `length_mmi=45, length_taper=10, gap_mmi=1.0, width_taper=1.0`; `straight_heater_metal(length)` matches the heater span; a coupler is placed by its single port. The router then sees the original port positions and spacings.
- 2026-10-05 (implementation): a net cell's region must come from one layer, the first core layer it draws on, not the union: the second waveguide layer of three of the files is a 3 um wide strip along the 0.5 um core, and merging the two swallowed the end facets and fused neighbouring nets (bs_circuit fell from 118 ports to 4). The one place the union was meant for, the sender-and-receiver circuit's 0.44 um gaps where a width transition is drawn on the second layer only, is closed instead by a morphological closing of the net region (`ExtractionConfig.net_gap_um`, 0.5 for that PDK, off by default; it must stay below the smallest coupling gap of a file).
- 2026-10-05 (implementation): three kinds of black box. The MMI of three of the files has no core shapes and its marker layer holds label fragments that do not fill the outline, so "touches the component's shapes" never fires: a black box (no shapes on the core layers) takes every end cap inside or on its bounding box as a port, and its other layers' shapes are ignored (the heater show box's metal would otherwise claim the bend segments under it). The thermo-optic heater of the sender-and-receiver and second Clements files exists only as such a show box (a show cell whose name also carries the net-name marker `_to_` and must be classified before the nets); the first Clements mesh's phase shifter is a black box with its ends on its outline.
- 2026-10-05 (implementation): an end cap is a facet whose neighbours run perpendicular to it, judged on chords of at least 0.2 um: a bend's polygonized arc and the tiny steps a sizing leaves behind are short hull edges too, and without the test they became ports of black boxes and open ends.
- 2026-10-05 (implementation): the first Clements mesh's I/O. On its left and middle edges a waveguide ends exactly on an empty end-cap cell with an empty edge-coupler cell 22 um behind it (photonic wire bond sites): 18 nets ending on an end cap. On its right edge the nets end in free space, 34 um from the edge-coupler cells: by their names they lead to a polarization splitter-rotator (two waveguides 2 um apart per channel) and an auxiliary coupler per channel, neither drawn in the file. `ExtractionConfig.open_ends` turns such a free end cap into a one-port `open_end` component (18 of them). End caps and open ends are rebuilt as a 10 um generic `straight` whose `o1` sits at the waveguide's end, and the nets to them are routed like any other: the route to the chip edge is real routing work.
- 2026-10-05 (implementation): gdsfactory resolves an `Instance`'s component name to the underlying cell function on validation (`straight_heater_metal` becomes `straight_heater_metal_undercut`, `grating_coupler_te` becomes `grating_coupler_elliptical_trenches`) and fills every default into its settings; the generated modules carry those resolved names and full settings and activate the generic PDK in `build_schematic` like the other benchmarks. The ring circuit's rings have one bus each (two ports): `ring_single(length_x = span - 26)` reproduces the port span (measured on the generic PDK: o1 at -13 - length_x, o2 at 13); a four-port ring would get `ring_double`.
- 2026-10-05 (routing, Milestone 3): three router findings on the rebuilt circuits, each pinned by experiment. (1) Die room: the generic couplers are 26 um deep where the foundry ones were 430 um, and a loop from the top to the bottom coupler of a column runs around the column's back as it did in the original; without `--chip-add-x/y-um 100` the bs_circuit run lost 8 nets. (2) Bend radius: every MZI arm of the four MZI circuits leaves its MMI 20 um before the heater input, 30 to 45 um beside it; with 10 um bends the S-bend of two quarter arcs fits exactly and the grid router crossed its own path (ring_circuit, 4 nets) or left the endpoint correction no straight segment (clements_2, 252 verifier errors); 5 um bends route every one of them. The generic heater's contact via stacks (16 um past each waveguide end) blocked the same connections and are dropped (`via_stack=None`). (3) Grid phase at a 2 um port pitch: the MMI's two input ports (2 um apart in three of the files, 1.33 um in the first Clements mesh) land in the same 2 um grid cell for some grid phases, and then the second net can never reach its target cell -- "No route found" after a full-grid search, "corridor target region size 1" in the diagnostics, independent of clearance, keepouts, fan-out mode and net order. A two-net cut-out (the straight into o1 and the U-turn out of o2) fails on the 2 um grid and routes on a 1 um grid; shifting the grid by 1 um in y moves the failure to another port pair. The private circuits therefore route on `--grid-size-um 1.0`. (4) Net order: on the 1 um grid the sender-and-receiver circuit still lost the same in-line coupler-to-MMI straight, now because a net declared earlier was routed first and its route walled the MMI's port lane in, and the repair never ripped it up (a two-net cut-out routes; with the pair declared first or last the whole circuit routes). The rebuild now writes the nets in the order of increasing port distance and the flags add `--net-order topological-span` (shortest first within the topological layers), which the first Clements mesh needs as well: in declaration order it stopped at net 170 of 240 with a non-converging repair, shortest-first routes all 240. (5) The first Clements mesh's three end caps at the end of a 180-degree turn out of an MMI (10 um radius in the original) fail the endpoint correction with 5 um bends ("no axis-aligned centerline segment is suitable", the last straight before the stub is too short) and route with 4 or 6 um; the circuit's flag override uses 4.

## Decision Log

- 2026-10-05 (owner): the five circuits of the dump are the next benchmarks; confidential, never into git; rebuilt with the open-source PDK elements; photonic routing only for now, since the GDS files carry no electrical routing to compare against.
- 2026-10-05 (lead): what lives where. In the repository, committed: the extractor module (generic: a configuration of name patterns, layers and composite cells drives it), the generator script, the unit tests on a synthetic GDS that the tests build themselves from generic components, the `.gitignore` entry for `benchmarks/private/`, this plan and the docs. Outside git, in `benchmarks/private/` (git-ignored, with its own `__init__.py` so `python -m photonic_router route private.<name>` imports `benchmarks.private.<name>`): the five configurations (they name foundry cells), the generated benchmark modules, and nothing else; the GDS files stay in the dump and are read in place by path. The plan and the state file name the circuits by the owner's file names and the counts the extraction reports, nothing more.
- 2026-10-05 (lead): components are placed by their ports. For every original component instance the extractor records its ports (position and outward normal from the facets, or the placeholder's origin); the generic replacement is parameterized to reproduce the port geometry (the MMI's taper and gap, the heater's length) and placed by the rigid transform that maps its port set onto the original's (rotation from a port normal, mirror from the original instance's mirror flag, translation from one port). Port names are matched by position in the component's own frame (left or right side, then upper or lower). This keeps every port where the original router had it; the component bodies differ, which the router sees only as obstacles.
- 2026-10-05 (lead): routing nets only. Every original waveguide piece (routed waveguides, s-bends, delay lines, u-turns) becomes a net to route afresh; intentional delay lines are therefore not preserved. Path-length matching is off for these benchmarks (no `NODE_TYPES` contract from the owner; the Clements meshes are balanced by construction). Crossings: off unless a circuit's nets cross by topology, which the first routing run shows.
- 2026-10-05 (lead): bond pads and heater metal are extracted (kind and position) but not emitted into the benchmarks until the owner wants electrical routing on them.


## Context and Orientation

The dump directory holds, besides two cascade diagrams, five GDS files: a beam-splitter circuit (2024 tape-out, 4.5 x 1.8 mm; 16 MMI2x2, 20 heaters of 146 um, 14 grating couplers and 2 two-port couplers, about 60 nets), a sender-and-receiver circuit (same PDK, 2.8 x 1.7 mm; 24 MMI2x2, 15 heaters of 100 um, 24 island couplers, about 90 nets), a first Clements mesh (2025, 6.1 x 1.5 mm; 60 MMI2x2 of 75 um, 51 heaters of 300 um, 24 edge couplers and 42 end caps as empty placeholder cells, about 240 nets, port name texts on an annotation layer), a second Clements mesh (2025, 4.8 x 1.4 mm; 33 real MMI2x2 plus about 700 unconnected show cells, 24 heaters of 100 um, 16 island couplers, 12 end caps, about 140 nets) and a ring circuit (2022, 2.6 x 1.7 mm; 8 MMI2x2 of 151 um, 12 heaters of 216 um and 2 bent ones, 2 rings as container cells, 14 grating couplers, 24 bond pads, s-bends and delay lines as routing). Waveguide core layers differ per tape-out (8/0 with a second waveguide layer 9/0; 10/0; 85/0).

Repository side: `benchmarks/<name>.py` modules expose `build_schematic()` (a gdsfactory `Schematic` of `Instance`s with `Placement`s and `Net`s) plus optional `STABLE_ROUTING_FLAGS`; `python/photonic_router/flow.py::load_benchmark` imports `benchmarks.<name>`, so `private.<name>` resolves to `benchmarks/private/<name>.py`. `translation/layout_from_schematic.py` turns the schematic into the unrouted layout. `python/photonic_router/benchmark_extractor.py` is unrelated (it extracts obstacles from a gdsfactory component for the router). The cascade builder (`benchmarks/cascade.py`) shows how a generated benchmark looks; `tests/test_cascade_benchmarks.py` how one is tested.


## Milestone 1: the extractor

`translation/gds_circuit_extraction.py`: `ExtractionConfig` (core layers as a list of (layer, datatype); classification rules as ordered (kind, name substrings) pairs with `net` first; composite-component rules as (kind, container-cell substring); placeholder kinds; tolerances); `extract_circuit(gds_path, config) -> ExtractedCircuit` with `components` (name, kind, original cell name, absolute transform, ports: position, normal, local side) and `nets` (two endpoints as (component name, port index), plus the dropped stubs and the unresolved ends, each with a reason); `generic_schematic(extracted, mapping) -> Schematic` with the port-based placement and the parameterized generic components (`mapping`: kind -> generic component name and the parameters derived from the measured port geometry); `write_benchmark_module(extracted, path)` writing a module like the cascade ones (instances, placements, nets, `STABLE_ROUTING_FLAGS`). Steps inside `extract_circuit`: flatten the hierarchy to leaf instances with absolute `DCplxTrans`; classify; composite containers become one component each; merge the union of each net cell's waveguide layers into one region; for every merged polygon find its port facets (short edges touching a component's shapes, or within the placeholder tolerance of a placeholder's origin); union the polygon's facets into a net; classify nets by port count. The report (`ExtractedCircuit.report()`) lists counts per kind and every net that is not two-ported, so Milestone 2 can account for each.

Tests (`tests/test_gds_circuit_extraction.py`): a synthetic circuit built with generic components (two MMIs, a heater, four grating couplers) and gdsfactory routes, written to a GDS in `tmp_path` with the generic layer numbers; the extractor must recover the instances, their placements (within 1 nm) and the net endpoints exactly; a second case with a placeholder cell and a stub to an end cap. Acceptance: tests green; the module under 600 lines or split by concern.
