# Admissible A* heuristic for 45-degree routing: weight 1.25 -> 1.0 unblocks multiportmmi_16x16

This ExecPlan is a living document. The sections `Progress`, `Surprises & Discoveries`, `Decision Log`, and `Outcomes & Retrospective` must be kept up to date as work proceeds. This document must be maintained in accordance with `.agent/PLANS.md`.

## Purpose / Big Picture

`multiportmmi_16x16` under its documented stable baseline (`--crossings true --crossing-mode lidar-pure --fanout-access-mode static-stubs`, applied automatically as CLI defaults by `routing_flow.py`) had never routed to completion. The failure history walked from `n_50` to `n_49` to, most recently, `n_123` -- two adjacent nets wanting nearly identical 45-degree diagonals into adjacent staggered stub anchors, which the repair loop cannot untangle (`.agent/execplans/2026-08-26-target-side-static-stubs-for-dense-mmi-ports.md`, Progress entry of 2026-08-26). Separately, the repository owner observed visibly wasted geometry in routed GDSs: routes overshooting past their target column and coming back, and a needless 45/45 chicane on even the very first, completely unobstructed net of a dense MMI group.

Both observations turned out to share one root cause: for 45-degree routing, the router runs *Weighted A**, an A* variant whose heuristic is multiplied by a factor above 1 so the search greedily rushes toward the target. `translation/route_rust.py` (the Python driver that configures the Rust A* backend) clamps `heuristic_weight` to at least 1.25 whenever `allow_45_degree_turns` is true. A weighted heuristic is *inadmissible* -- it can overestimate the remaining cost -- so A* loses its optimality guarantee and may return paths up to 25 percent worse than the best one. That is exactly what the GDS showed, and, at the scale of a dense fan-in, those suboptimal greedy paths collide with each other in ways the repair loop cannot fix.

After this change, the clamp minimum is overridable: `PHOTONIC_ROUTER_MIN_HEURISTIC_WEIGHT=1.0` selects ordinary, admissible A* for 45-degree routing; unset, everything behaves exactly as before (1.25). The observable outcome: from the repository root, `PHOTONIC_ROUTER_MIN_HEURISTIC_WEIGHT=1.0 PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python routing_flow.py multiportmmi_16x16` routes all 223 nets and writes `build/routed_multiportmmi_16x16.gds`, with both `build/verification/multiportmmi_16x16_photonic_verification.json` and `..._crossing_verification.json` reporting `success: true, error_count: 0, warning_count: 0` -- the first fully clean run of this benchmark ever recorded in this repository. The reason it is not simply the new default is the validation ladder: at 1.0, `multiportmmi_8x8` and `benes_16x16` each expose a latent defect of their own (details in Progress), so flipping the default is an open owner decision.

## Surprises & Discoveries

- Observation: the wasted geometry the owner flagged from the GDS was not blocking, congestion, or net ordering -- the very first net of the group, routed into an empty region, already carried the signature chicane. A single-net witness (`--debug-stop-after-route`) made the cause bisectable in minutes; the full-benchmark view had suggested ordering effects that were not the mechanism.
- Observation: the per-net diagnostics file already contained the disproof. `target_bend_footprint=label=end_from_below_to_port; ...; static_blockers=[]; dynamic_blockers=[]` documents that the cheaper arrival was legal; no new instrumentation was needed, only reading what `_write_route_diagnostics` (`translation/route_rust.py`) already emits.
- **Observation: one inadmissible-heuristic clamp explained four documented defects at once** -- the `n_123` adjacent-diagonal repair exhaustion (the hard blocker for this benchmark) and the three fanout-region defects `n_15`/`n_16`/`n_74`. None of these were individually mis-diagnosed: the adjacent-diagonal contention is real, but admissible search finds the escape paths that greedy search never expands, so the contention never hardens into repair exhaustion.
- Observation: the feared runtime cost did not materialize at benchmark scale here: 182 s total for the full 16x16, versus roughly 2 minutes to *fail* at weight 1.25, and well under `benes_16x16`'s 392 s baseline. The 25x state-expansion factor measured on the single witness net does not translate into 25x wall time because most nets are simple (122 of 223 took the simple-route path) and the expensive nets were already expensive (n_74: 2.15M expansions in both configurations).
- Observation: Python's stdout is block-buffered when redirected to a file, so a monitor polling the log of a running `routing_flow.py` sees nothing until exit; judge liveness by process CPU, not log growth.

## Outcomes & Retrospective

The owner's by-eye reading of the GDS ("this path overshoots, there is no reason for it, and it blocks nothing") was correct on every count and led, through a single-net witness, to a one-line root cause: the 1.25 heuristic-weight clamp makes 45-degree A* deliberately suboptimal. At 1.0 the long-blocked `multiportmmi_16x16` routes completely clean for the first time ever -- and three separately-documented defects (`n_15`/`n_16`/`n_74`) vanish with it -- but the ladder exposes the other side of the trade: two latent holes that weight 1.25 happened never to trigger (a parallel-diagonal overlap the grid legality model cannot see on `multiportmmi_8x8`, and an endpoint-correction failure of the known `no realizable centerline` class on `benes_16x16`), plus a 2.2x slowdown on `benes_8x8`. The change is committed env-gated and off by default; the open owner decisions are recorded in the Decision Log and in `.agent/REPOSITORY_STATE.md`.

Lessons: (1) a "performance tuning" constant that changes *which* paths are found is not a tuning constant -- it is part of the correctness envelope, and every downstream mechanism (repair, legality, endpoint correction, iteration budgets) had silently equilibrated around the 1.25 paths; (2) the single-net witness (`--debug-stop-after-route` on the first net of a group) turned a benchmark-scale mystery into a minutes-scale bisection, and the per-net diagnostics file already contained the decisive evidence (`target_bend_footprint ... static_blockers=[]`); (3) verification layers are not redundant: the crossing verification was green over a 70 um physical overlap that only the photonic gate caught.

## Context and Orientation

Routing in this repository is grid A* in Rust (`src/astar.rs`) driven from Python (`translation/route_rust.py`). A route is a sequence of primitives (straights, 45- and 90-degree bends from `src/primitives.rs`); each primitive's step cost is its physical length plus `bend_weight * bend_cost`, where `bend_cost` counts eighth-turns (a 45-degree bend is 1, a 90-degree bend is 2). The A* heuristic (`SearchHeuristic` in `src/astar.rs`) estimates remaining cost as Euclidean (or octile, in `diagonal_aware` mode) distance plus a minimum-bend term, and multiplies the whole estimate by `config.heuristic_weight`. A weight of exactly 1.0 keeps the estimate at or below the true remaining cost ("admissible"), which is the precondition for A* returning cheapest paths. Any weight above 1.0 trades path quality for search speed.

The clamp lives in `RouteNetsSession` setup in `translation/route_rust.py` (search for `heuristic_weight`): when `allow_45_degree_turns` is true it raises `astar_cfg.heuristic_weight` to at least the clamp value before handing the config to the Rust backend. 90-degree-only configurations (for example `heater_s_mod`'s stable flags) never hit this clamp.

`routing_flow.py <benchmark>` applies each benchmark's `STABLE_ROUTING_FLAGS`/`STABLE_ROUTING_ENV` (declared in `benchmarks/<name>.py`) as CLI defaults; explicit flags still win. Reproductions of failure states must use the real CLI, not direct `run_routing_flow(...)` calls -- those were observed to fail at a different net under nominally identical flags (see the 2026-08-26 target-side-stubs plan's decision log).
