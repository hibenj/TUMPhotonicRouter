## Purpose / Big Picture

After the optical routing, the flow can route each heater's two electrical terminals: one to a bondpad in a row above the die, the other into a common bus along the bottom that returns to its own pad. On `heater_s_mod` (21 heaters, 22 pads) this works and verifies clean, but the metal looks wrong in three ways the owner named from the rendered layout: pad wires descend in staircases of one-pitch jogs instead of a single L or Z, wires enter their terminal contacts through small extra elbows, and the common bus is a 81-bend Steiner tree whose stubs zigzag and whose junctions are drawn as overlapping rectangles. Separately, the electrical benchmark suite's guardrail check has been red on every case since June (Decision Log D11 of the restructure plan): four commits added pads, contacts and a bus bondpad to the metal the metrics count, and the guardrail limits were never re-derived.

After this plan: the suite check is green with guardrails that state real properties (no same-net metal drawn twice, wire length equal to the Manhattan distance unless an obstacle forces more); every pad wire on `heater_s_mod` is an L or a Z with zero detour, terminals are entered straight, the bus is a trunk with straight stubs and merged junctions; `translation/electrical` (8,100 lines in 17 modules today) is a pipeline of named stages with typed inputs and outputs, each with its own tests, documented in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`. The optical routing and the 27 paper cells are untouched: the electrical step runs after the waveguides are realized and verified, and no paper configuration enables it.

## Surprises & Discoveries

- 2026-09-25: the one "same-net redundant overlap" on `heater_s_mod` (a 20 x 5 um strip, 100 um2 raw, 66.7 um2 after the metric's attribution) was first read as a branch running into the escape; measured, it is the reverse: the escape's first rectangle extended 200 um back into the bus stripe (its start kept the half-width end cap every open wire end gets), so the strip where a branch legitimately pokes into the stripe was covered twice. A real geometry slip, on all four cases, fixed in Milestone 1.
- 2026-09-25: the size guardrails (length, raw and union area, overcount) were pinned before the metrics counted pad markers, terminal contacts and the bus bondpad; the ten-fold area jump is the pads (a 22-pad row dwarfs the wires), not worse wires. A limit on "raw metal area" that mixes pads with wires cannot state anything about routing quality.
- 2026-09-25 (Milestone 2, first attempt): with L wires in place the old Z mechanism turned out to be unsafe: its lane prefix was placed by a fixed projection with no collision check (only the pad-stub leg was searched), which had been harmless while every wire of a bundle used the same coordinated lanes. On `heater_s_mod` the one heater whose pad lies on the wrong side of its exit (`heater_extra_0`) fell back to a Z whose prefix ran through the pad columns of the neighbouring group's lower rows; without the implementer's safety check it drew shorting metal, with it three wires failed. Decision: the fallback is a full grid search over obstacles and committed wires, run after every L is committed; the lane and shelf machinery leaves the pad-wire path (deleted in Milestone 4).
- 2026-09-25 (Milestone 2, second attempt): with the searched fallback, two wires of the four-row post group still had no legal path at all (checked by an exhaustive breadth-first search over the blocked cells). The render showed why: the lone heater right of that group (`heater_extra_0`) had its pad on its right but its individual terminal exiting left, so its pad wire ran through the pad channel and down the corridor between the group and its own body, walling off the lower rows' pads from below. The terminal roles of a lone heater must follow the side its pad lies on (pad wire from the terminal facing the pad, bus branch from the other); with that, the corridor (about 5 lanes) and the channel (4 lanes) carry the group's Zs. The lane machinery of the old design was structurally the right river-routing picture; its flaws were the uncoordinated pad assignment and the blind prefix, not the shelves.
- 2026-09-25 (Milestone 2, third attempt): with the lone heater's terminals swapped, the post group's second row became an L too, and that is the second flaw: an L for a middle row is a wall from its terminal row to the pad row, and the outer rows' pads (which nesting places beyond it, above the neighbour's heater body) become unreachable from the corridor; an exhaustive search confirmed no legal path. Greedy L-first is not a river routing. Decision: each bundle is routed as a whole: corridor lanes ordered outward, channel lanes ordered downward, each wire monotone (L when its pad column is its corridor lane, else a 3-bend Z), the bundle committed only if every segment is free, otherwise the per-wire search as the fallback.
- 2026-09-28 (Milestone 2, verification of the implementation): on `heater_lanes_20` the two wires with a detour (`heater_extra_0:r`, `heater_extra_3:l`, 20 um each, 2 bends) are the second rows of two-heater bundles whose pad column lies inward of their corridor lane; the river construction refuses such a bundle by design and the searched fallback serves it. The 15 um cross-net minimum on that case (20 um before) is the bus escape's rectangle against the pad of `heater_final_3`, a pad-placement distance at the 10 um required clearance; no wire is involved.
- 2026-09-28 (Milestone 2, the multi-heater fixture): `test_auto_pad_origin_compacts_row_toward_escape_topology` and `test_detailed_bundle_router_nests_l_and_z_pad_wires_from_topology` (20 um grid, 150 um pad pitch, zero obstacle clearance, pad origin forced to 0) had been flipped or weakened to accept failed verification. Measured: with the forced origin the per-wire fallback drew four pairs of sibling wires edge to edge, because committed wires were held apart by the obstacle clearance (0 there) instead of `individual_route_spacing_um`; with the automatic origin one wire clipped the bus escape, because only the escape's centerline was blocked while it is realized 400 um wide (the "dilated escape" set was an always-empty placeholder with two checks that never tripped); 11 of 20 forced wires had no legal path at all (exhaustive search) and each such fallback search took 6.7 s by exhausting every direction state of the grid, which put three tests at 41 s and the fast suite at 159 s against its 60 s limit. Fixed: a spacing radius (`_wire_spacing_radius_cells`) for committed wires, tested against the new wire's bare centerline; the escape blocked at its realized rectangles from the same function realization uses (`metal_realization.common_bus_escape_rects`); a plain reachability search before the fallback A*; off-grid river lanes refused instead of clamped. After the fix the automatic origin draws no overlap but cannot serve `heater_final_3` (its pad is compacted against the 400 um escape; on HEAD the wire clipped the escape undetected, so HEAD's clean verdict there was false), and the forced origin serves 10 of 20 wires without any positive-area overlap (at zero clearance the reservation radius lets metal touch a neighbouring pad edge to edge; the verifier reports touching as an overlap). - 2026-09-28 (Milestone 3, first attempt): a trunk in the ports' own column is impossible. The bus terminals of a column share the port's grid column, but that column lies inside the heater bodies' raw bounding boxes (the port sits at the body's edge), and every intervening member's open-cell disk hides its body from the cell-level check; the verifier's `metal_overlaps_raw_obstacle` (metal over a raw obstacle box outside the net's own contact boxes) reported 192 overlaps on `heater_lanes_20`. ## Milestone 2: pad wires without staircases or terminal hooks

Where the staircases come from (evidence lane, 2026-09-25, four wires traced stage by stage on `heater_s_mod`): no obstacle is involved. Each pad wire is built as terminal -> sideways onto a *lane column* (the bundle skeleton's column shifted by the wire's rank times the 40 um track pitch: `_attach_point_and_tail`, `_route_prefix_to_pad_stub_start`) -> up the lane column -> at a shelf height below the pad row (`_individual_pad_lane_point`, staggered by rank so shelves do not collide) sideways to the pad's column (`_route_pad_stub_path`, grid A*) -> up into the pad. The lane column is neither the terminal's column nor the pad's, so every wire pays two horizontal jogs, and whenever the second jog runs back toward the first, the difference is the detour (20 to 120 um). A skeleton corner near the terminal adds a fourth bend on some wires. The elbow at the terminal contact is the adapter `terminal_contacts.terminal_access_path` drawing a margin stub out of the port before joining the trimmed tail.

Design: a pad wire is an L: from the terminal's port exit, one horizontal run at the terminal's row to the assigned pad's column, then one vertical run straight into the pad. Terminals stacked in one column (the four rows of a heater group) get pads whose columns nest: the top row the nearest pad, the bottom row the farthest, so a lower wire's vertical run never crosses an upper wire's horizontal run (river routing). When the pad column is blocked between the terminal row and the pad row (an obstacle cell, a reserved wire), the wire falls back to a Z: horizontal at the terminal row to the nearest free column, vertical to the shelf, horizontal to the pad column, vertical into the pad, i.e. today's mechanism, with the shelf heights kept. The pad assignment inside a bundle is chosen for nesting first (`pad_slots`), then the L is tried, then the Z. The terminal adapter becomes a straight landing: the wire's first segment starts at the contact and runs in the port's exit direction; the adapter dogleg disappears when the wire leaves in that direction.

Work: implement the L-first construction in `bundle_detail_router` (a new function per wire that returns the L or None, called before the lane/stub path; the Z path unchanged as the fallback), the nesting order in `pad_slots` (or in `_pad_lane_rank_maps` if the assignment order is decided there), the straight landing in `terminal_contacts`/`metal_realization`. Tests: on the three heater fixtures and `heater_s_mod`, every pad wire has detour 0 and at most 2 bends unless the test names the blocked column; a fixture with an obstacle above a terminal forces the Z and asserts its bend count and that the L was refused; the nesting test asserts no two wires of a bundle cross (segment intersection over the centerlines). Guardrails tightened: `pad_wire_max_bend_count` 2 (3 for the fixture with the blocked column), `pad_wire_detour_total_um` 0.

Acceptance: the per-wire table on `heater_s_mod` all zeros in the detour column and no wire above 2 bends; cross-net spacing unchanged (`cross_net_min_spacing_um` >= 20); the suite check green with the tightened limits; the rendered metal reviewed by the owner.


## Milestone 3: the common bus as straight column trunks

Where the bus's bends come from (evidence lane, 2026-09-25, `heater_s_mod`): the stripe is fine (400 um wide, trimmed to its used span) and the escape is one L. The 21 branches are the problem: in each four-row heater group the router picks the inner terminal of every row (`:r` in the left column, `:l` in the right one) and runs each branch as an arm of 9 to 11 cells to a shared trunk column midway between the two columns, then down; 16 such branches, two bends each. The remaining five branches drop straight when their column is free and zigzag by one pitch when it is not. The obstruction is never a heater body or a waveguide (the obstacle scan below every representative terminal is empty); it is the rule in `common_bus_router._forbidden_terminal_cells` that a branch may not pass through another terminal's open-cell disk, even though every terminal the common bus selects belongs to the same net: heater_0's straight drop would pass through heater_1, heater_2 and heater_3's bus terminals, which is precisely what a bus should do. Realization already merges same-net rectangles per net before writing the layer (`_append_merged_rects` through a klayout region), so the doubled outlines in the debug SVG are the pre-merge rectangles, not what the GDS contains.

Design: the bus branches become column trunks.
