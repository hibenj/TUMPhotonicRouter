## Purpose / Big Picture

Every heater has one individual pad wire from its terminal to a bond pad in the pad row above the layout. Since the channel routing plan (`.agent/execplans/2026-09-29-pad-wire-channel-routing.md`, complete, commit 0bd1a75) every such wire is one horizontal run at some track height between two vertical runs: its corridor lane (a fixed column beside its heater, one lane per row of the bundle) and its pad column. The pads themselves are still placed by the old rules: one contiguous block of pads per escape bundle, on the bundle's exit side, anchored outward of its first lane, the blocks pushed apart along the row. That choice makes the wires long and the channel high: on `cascade_16_folded` an eight-row bundle's pads stretch over 1,040 um on one side, the longest horizontal run is 810 um, eleven tracks are needed (channel 350 um high), and the pad row has 45 empty slots between its first and last pad. The owner's direction (2026-09-29): the pads are not yet chosen to minimize the routing; that is this plan.

After this plan the pad plan chooses every individual pad by one general rule: the pads are assigned to the wires in the order of their corridor lanes, with one pad per slot of the pad pitch, so that the sum of the horizontal runs (the distance between each wire's lane and its pad column) is as small as possible; the pad row's origin is chosen the same way. A measured prototype of exactly that rule on the routed lanes (2026-10-05, script in the session scratchpad, reproduced in Milestone 1's tests) gives: `heater_s_mod` 4 tracks to 2, run sum 4,230 um to 1,820 um; `cascade_12_folded` 7 tracks to 3, 7,230 um to 4,030 um; `cascade_16_folded` 11 tracks to 4, 14,680 um to 7,020 um, longest run 810 um to 400 um; `cascade_16_diagonal` 10 tracks to 4, 14,160 um to 6,750 um; no wire in a constraint cycle anywhere. To see it working: `python -m photonic_router route cascade_16_folded` prints its `SUMMARY` line with `electrical_ok=True`, and the electrical metrics (`scripts/benchmark_electrical.py cascade_16_folded`) show `pad_channel_height_um` 140 instead of 350 and a smaller `centerline_length_um`.

## Surprises & Discoveries

- 2026-10-05 (prototype): the first run of the prototype packed every pad into a slot range that was too short for the lane span and produced runs of 2 mm and twenty tracks; with a slot range covering every lane plus a margin the same rule gives the numbers above. Lesson for Milestone 1: the candidate slots must cover the whole lane span plus at least the number of wires on each side, and a test must pin that a wire's pad never ends more than a few pitches from its lane when slots are free.
- 2026-10-05 (Milestone 2, pitch in cells): the first integration modelled the slots as `round(pitch / grid)` cells apart; on the multi-heater fixture (150 um pitch, 20 um grid, 7.5 cells) the model drifted by a cell every other slot, 400 um over forty slots, and a whole bundle got its pads on the wrong side. The search now reads each candidate slot's column through the same chain the router reads (`_make_slot` -> `pad_access_cells` -> `pad_column_cell`); the cascades (130 um on 10 um, exact) were unaffected.
- 2026-10-05 (Milestone 2, channel headroom): with pads on both sides of a bundle, a run on the lowest track routinely passes over the heater column; on the 20 um grid that run's reservation (radius `wire_reservation_radius_cells` = 1 cell) touched the layout's top row (obstacle cells at y 57 under a track at y 58) and four row-1 wires were refused, their fallbacks failing too. The channel used to be one wire width above the layout -- physically exact (half width plus clearance) but one cell short in the cell model. `_required_pad_channel_offset_um` now adds `(radius + 1)` cells instead of the wire width: 10 um more on the 10 um grid (the cascades' channels 80 -> 90, 110 -> 120, 140 -> 150), 20 um more on the 20 um grid.
- 2026-10-05 (the detour metric): `pad_wire_detour_total_um` rose on every suite case (heater_s_mod 0 -> 860 um) while the wires got shorter (centerline 25,110 -> 21,590 um). The metric (`metrics.py`) is a wire's length minus the Manhattan distance from its port to its pad; a pad inward of the wire's lane counts the lane excursion twice although the lane is forced by the row and the wire is no longer than with the same pad outward. The guardrails were re-pinned at the measured values; whether the metric should become "length minus (stub + run + vertical)", i.e. zero for every channel-routed wire, is the owner's call (Decision Log).
- 2026-10-05 (a cycle never happened): on no case did the monotone assignment produce a constraint cycle; the per-wire fallback is unreached on every benchmark and fixture (its tests force one wire out of the channel result, from the channel routing plan).


## Decision Log

- 2026-09-29 (owner): fix the routing generally, not with special cases; the pad-choice rules are the next item after the channel routing plan ("pads are not yet chosen to minimize the routing").
- 2026-10-05 (lead, for the owner's confirmation): the objective is the sum over all wires of the horizontal run, the distance in x between a wire's corridor lane and its pad column. Reasons: every wire's vertical length is fixed by its row and the pad row (the sum of its two vertical runs is the pad edge minus the terminal row, whatever the track), so the horizontal run is the only part of the wire length the pad choice controls; and the track count is a consequence of the runs (a track is shared by runs that do not overlap, so short runs mean few tracks: measured 11 to 4 on `cascade_16_folded`). The track count is not optimized directly; it is measured and pinned. If the owner prefers the channel height first, the pure function is the one place to change.
- 2026-10-05 (lead): the pads of all wires are assigned in the order of their corridor lanes (monotone). This is not a preference but a necessity of the one-metal-layer channel: two wires whose pad order differs from their lane order always have overlapping runs (either nested, or each one's lane inside the other's run), and both overlaps are constraint cycles of `river_routing.assign_tracks` (one wire must lie above the other and below it), which send a wire to the per-wire fallback. With the monotone order the constraints form chains only, and no wire falls back (measured: zero cycles on every case).
- 2026-10-05 (lead, for the owner's confirmation): a pad may lie on either side of its bundle's lanes, including inward, over the heater column itself. The eight-row-bundles plan already produced inward blocks (its "inward nesting", 2026-09-28) and the owner accepted those pads; with the monotone rule an eight-row bundle gets four pads on each side of its lane band, which is what halves the runs. The pad row is above the layout, so nothing of the layout lies under any pad; the bus pad keeps its end position and its escape gap (`common_bus_pad_slot_gap`).
- 2026-10-05 (lead): the pad row's origin is chosen by the same objective: the assignment is computed for every origin offset within one pad pitch at the grid resolution (thirteen offsets at the 130 um pitch on the 10 um grid) and the best sum wins; a forced `pad_origin_x_um` fixes the origin and keeps the assignment rule.
- 2026-10-05 (lead): the assignment is exact, by dynamic programming over (wire, slot) with a strictly increasing slot index and the configured empty slots between assignments; at most a few hundred slots and fifty wires, so cost is no concern. No heuristic.
- 2026-10-05 (lead, correcting the line above about the forced origin): a forced `pad_origin_x_um` keeps the old sequential placement (pads from the first index in topology order, one per stride), not the assignment rule -- it is the owner's explicit placement and the forced-origin tests pin it.
- Open (owner): the objective and the two-sided pads (the two lead decisions above).
- Open (owner): the detour metric's definition (Surprises); the re-pinned detour guardrails say nothing about wire quality until it is settled.

## Outcomes & Retrospective

The individual pads are chosen by one general rule: pads in corridor-lane order, one per slot, minimizing the sum of the horizontal runs, the origin by the same measure (`pad_slots.assign_pad_slots`, `assign_pad_slots_with_origin`); a multi-row bundle gets half its pads on each side of its lane band. The eight-row-bundles plan's interval machinery (blocks, anchors, pushes, Rule A') is gone, `pad_slots.py` is shorter with the new functions than before without them. All six cascades verify clean with 2 to 4 tracks (were 4 to 11), every wire channel-routed, runs halved, `cascade_16_folded`'s channel 350 -> 150 um and its pad wires 97,600 -> 80,750 um of centerline; the suite re-pinned with every case cleaner except the detour metric, whose definition is now the open item. Three things the prototype did not show and the integration did: the slot pitch must be read in real geometry, not rounded cells; the lowest track needs the reservation radius over the layout once runs cross the heater column; the detour metric penalizes inward pads by construction. Lesson: a pure function proven on one grid is not proven on another; the fixture at 20 um found two of the three. ## Context and Orientation

Terms. The pad row is a line of bond pads above (`pad_side="top"`) or below the layout; a slot is one legal pad position on that line, at `origin_x + index * pad_pitch_um` (130 um by default), and a pad plan (`translation/electrical/pad_slots.py::plan_pad_slots`, stage 6 of `translation/electrical/route_electrical.py`) assigns every individual terminal and the common bus to a slot without making geometry. A wire's corridor lane is the column `river_routing.corridor_lane_column(opening_edge, exit_dx, row, pitch, clearance)` beside its heater: the bundle's opening edge (its outermost open terminal cell on the exit side, `river_routing.bundle_opening_edge`) plus the clearance plus the row number times the track pitch (30 um), outward. Row 1 is the terminal nearest the pad row; its lane is the innermost. The lane order of a bundle is forced (a nearer row on an outer lane would cross a farther row's stub), so the only freedom is the pad. A wire's pad column is the center column of its pad's access cells (`river_routing.pad_column_cell`). The wire's horizontal run spans from its lane to its pad column at a track height assigned by `river_routing.assign_tracks`, which is pure and runs twice: in the pad plan to size the channel and in the dispatcher (`bundle_detail_router.route_detailed_bundles`) to route.

Today's pad choice (`pad_slots.py`): `_individual_assignment_items` lists the terminals in escape-topology order (bundle by bundle along the pad row, rows in order); `_pad_intervals` makes one interval per bundle centered on the bundle's own column; `_assign_interval_indices` gives each interval one contiguous block of slots, pushed apart in order; `_resolve_origin_x` picks the origin as the median of the intervals' wishes; `_assign_interval_indices_pass2` re-anchors each block's row-1 pad outward of the lane c_1 (`_row_one_anchored_start`, `_row_one_corridor_x_um`) and pushes again; `_common_bus_pad_slot_index` puts the bus pad at the configured end with the escape gap; `_pad_channel_track_count` sizes the channel from the track count. Measured on 2026-10-05 (pads on the exit side only, blocks pushed): `heater_s_mod` 21 wires, 4 tracks, runs 4,230 um, no inward pad, slots 0..48 with 28 empty; `cascade_12_folded` 33 wires, 7 tracks, 7,230 um, 4 inward pads, slots 1..68 with 35 empty; `cascade_16_folded` 45 wires, 11 tracks, 14,680 um, longest run 810 um, 5 inward pads, slots 0..89 with 45 empty; `cascade_16_diagonal` 45 wires, 10 tracks, 14,160 um, slots 0..135 with 91 empty. The inward pads come from the pushes, not from a choice, and they are what forces the extra tracks (a pushed lone heater's lane within a pitch of a neighbour's pad column).

What stays: `PadPlan`, `PadSlot`, `PadAssignment` (with `topology_bundle_id` and `topology_rank`, read by the dispatcher), `common_bus_pad_slot_gap`, `_common_bus_pad_slot_index`, `_make_slot`, `pad_access_bbox`, `pad_access_cells`, `_required_pad_channel_offset_um`, `_pad_channel_track_count`, the `pad_origin_x_um`, `pad_empty_slots_between_assignments`, `pad_extra_slots_left/right` and `common_bus_pad_position` fields and their meaning. What goes: `_PadInterval`, `_pad_intervals`, `_make_interval`, `_assign_interval_indices`, `_interval_preferred_start`, `_interval_reference_index`, `_assign_interval_indices_pass2`, `_row_one_anchored_start`, `_row_one_corridor_x_um`, `_resolve_origin_x`, `_preferred_pad_x_by_terminal_id`, `_median_float`.


## Milestone 1: the pure assignment

`pad_slots.assign_pad_slots(lane_xs, slot_xs, gap_slots) -> list[int]`: `lane_xs` the wires' lanes in cells, sorted ascending (the caller sorts and keeps the permutation); `slot_xs` the candidate slot columns in cells, ascending, one per slot index; `gap_slots` the configured empty slots between two assigned pads (so consecutive chosen indices differ by at least `gap_slots + 1`). Returns one slot index per wire, strictly increasing, minimizing the sum of `abs(lane - slot_xs[index])`. Dynamic programming: `best[i][k]` the least cost of placing wires 0..i with wire i on slot k, `best[i][k] = abs(lane_i - slot_k) + min over j <= k - gap - 1 of best[i-1][j]`, with a running minimum over j so the whole table costs wires times slots; the argument of the minimum is kept for the backtrack. Raises `ValueError` when fewer slots than wires (times the gap) are offered.

`pad_slots.assign_pad_slots_with_origin(lane_xs, grid, config, forced_origin_x_um | None) -> (origin_x_um, indices)`: builds the candidate slots for one origin (from `lane_min - (n + extra_left + 2) * pitch` to `lane_max + (n + extra_right + 2) * pitch`, pitch in cells, so the slots always cover every lane with room for every wire on either side -- the prototype's lesson), runs the assignment, and, when the origin is free, repeats for every offset of the origin within one pitch at the grid resolution and keeps the best sum (ties: the smallest offset). Indices are then shifted so the first assigned index is `pad_extra_slots_left` (the plan's convention: `first_assignment_index`), which only moves the origin.

Tests (`tests/electrical/test_pad_slots.py`): one bundle of eight lanes three cells apart at a thirteen-cell pitch gets four pads on each side of its band and every run at most 400 um (the measured `cascade_16_folded` bundle); two facing bundles (a right-exiting one and a left-exiting one 100 cells apart) share the slots between them in lane order; a gap of one empty slot is honoured; the raised error; the origin search beats a fixed origin on an asymmetric lane set. Acceptance: the tests pass; nothing else changes yet (`plan_pad_slots` untouched in this milestone).
