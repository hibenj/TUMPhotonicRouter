Status: DESIGN DRAFT (2026-09-07). Nothing implemented. The owner decides whether this design replaces the compact lattice of `2026-08-26-preplaced-crossing-grids-for-benes.md`.

## Purpose / Big Picture

Owner direction (2026-09-07, after contribution 2 reached every Benes size with the compact lattice): re-explore the crossing structure, also for Benes. It should be "well structured and distributed over the free space in the layer, so that the cost function is still minimized, so paths should be minimized still", and it should apply to the multiportmmi benchmarks, whose layers are just as well defined (input, splitter tree, MMI, phase shifters, heaters, ...).

The compact lattice cannot do that. It is a block in the middle of the band: every lane detours from its natural row to a lattice slot and back, which costs length, and at multiportmmi it does not even fit (see the numbers in Surprises). This plan proposes the opposite: the structure is the set of shortest-path lanes themselves, and the crossing components sit where those shortest paths cross. The plan is a design with feasibility evidence first; no generator code before the owner has seen a drawn sample.

## Surprises & Discoveries

- The compact lattice's width is `levels x pitch` because it spends one column per swap level, and a lane that crosses k partners needs k levels. At multiportmmi the longest chains are 4 (mm8), 8 (mm16) and 16 (mm32) crossings on one lane; the lattice would be 148 / 308 / 628 um wide at pitch 14 (113 / 233 / 473 at the smallest legal pitch 9) against bands of 110 / 230 / 470 um -- and the band must also hold the fan from the 5 um MMI port pitch to the 100 um heater pitch. The lattice is the wrong shape for these layers, not merely too large.
- In the octile metric every path of the form `diagonal (d1) - vertical - diagonal (d2)` with `d1 + d2 = min(|dy|, band)` has the same, minimal length: `band + |dy| - (d1 + d2)(2 - sqrt 2)`. So the column (x of the vertical) of a long lane is a free parameter that costs nothing, and a short lane (`|dy| <= band`) is one diagonal whose start x is free. This is why a distributed structure can keep the paths minimal: it only chooses free parameters.
- lidar-pure's benes32 solutions include staircases (2026-09-03 E1: net 57 with five short verticals) -- the router itself gives up octile optimality where the columns get dense. The structure will have to allow the same fallback (a second column) or fail loudly on that lane.

## Decision Log

- Proposed (2026-09-07, lead): the structure is the set of shortest-path lanes -- each lane `[horizontal] diagonal [vertical] diagonal [horizontal]` between its natural source and target rows, one free parameter per lane (its column), crossings only at 90 degrees (diagonal-vs-antidiagonal on the chamfers, vertical-vs-horizontal for same-direction pairs and stationary lanes). Every other contact (45-degree, parallel overlap, crossing footprints closer than the crossing pitch, columns closer than the crossing pitch) is forbidden. A per-stage solver assigns the columns (ordered constraints from the pairwise crossing requirements, widest span first as the tiebreak, backtracking on spacing), exact shapely geometry validates the result before a component is emitted. The pre-wired stage component contains the lanes and the crossing cells; the router routes nothing inside a crossing layer and everything outside it as today.
