# Detour-aware routing: short nets first, and a pass that re-routes detoured nets


## Purpose / Big Picture

The optical router routes nets one after another and accepts any legal path, however long. On the owner's second Clements mesh (a private benchmark, see `.agent/execplans/2026-10-05-private-circuits-from-gds.md`) this produced a 457 um double spiral where a 55 um S-bend belongs: a long coupler net was routed first and ran through the interior of an MZI, the MZI's short feed then had to go around it, later repair rounds moved the coupler net away but never revisited the feed, and in the last repair round the two feeds of one arm ended up detouring around each other. Nothing in the geometry forces this; the same MZI alone routes all its feeds as 55 um S-bends.

After this change a user can (1) route with `--net-order span`, which routes every net in the order of increasing grid span regardless of the net graph's depth, so a 44 um MZI feed is placed before a 500 um coupler net, and (2) route with `--detour-pass-factor 3`, which after the normal routing marks every net whose route is longer than three times its free-space lower bound, rips all of them up together and routes them again, shortest first, against everything else; the stage prints how many nets it re-routed and by how much they shrank. On the second Clements mesh the two feeds of the affected arm come out at 54.7 um with either option; the paper cells, which have no such detours, route byte-identically because both options are off by default.

## Surprises & Discoveries

- 2026-10-06 (Milestone 1 evidence): `python -m photonic_router route heater_s_mod --net-order span` ends `SUMMARY benchmark=heater_s_mod nets=81 route_failures=0 verifier_errors=0 verifier_warnings=0 plm_groups=26 plm_over_tolerance=0 meander_inserted_um=23239.7 electrical_heaters=21 electrical_pads=22`; the default order inserts 23,582 um of meander on the same cell, so the new order changes route shapes slightly but routes and matches clean.

- 2026-10-06 (from the private-circuits plan, the evidence this plan rests on): the spiral is produced by the A* search itself (its grid path is 478 cells on a 1 um grid), not by the endpoint correction or any meander machinery. Static obstacles, port keepouts (`--foreign-port-keepout-cells 0` changes nothing) and port lanes are not involved. The feed is routed three times: first as a 501 um spiral around a coupler route that crosses the MZI interior at y 13242 (between the MMI output at y 13257 and the arm at y 13218), then, after a repair round rips it, as a clean 54.7 um S-bend, then, after another repair round, as the 457 um spiral around its sibling feed's 329 um loop; the coupler route has settled beneath the arm by then. Both feeds are legal and count as successes, so no later round touches them. Reproduced with `--debug-stop-after-route N` for N = 20, 21, 31, 36, 37 (`build/routed_private.clements_2_after_<N>_routes.gds`).

- 2026-10-06 (Milestone 2): the first version of the pass measured routes in router cells (`state.router.get_net_cells`) and selected all 200 nets of the second Clements mesh (329,838 cells, about 1,650 per net): that set is the committed footprint, route width plus clearance, not the centerline. Re-routing all 200 kept the verifier clean but took 173 s and defeats the purpose. The bookkeeping's `lengths_by_id` (micrometres, the length the GDS gets) is the right measure; with it the pass selects exactly the two feeds of the affected arm.


## Decision Log

- 2026-10-06 (owner): no smoothing or geometry post-processing. The remedy must stay inside the router's own logic: first try routing nearby, short nets first; and mark nets with detours and try, in a pass of their own at the end, to take them all out and route them again.
- 2026-10-06 (lead): both options are opt-in and off by default, so the nine paper cells of `gate_short` stay byte-identical and the electrical suite does not drift. The private circuits get the options in their `STABLE_ROUTING_FLAGS` once measured.
- 2026-10-06 (lead): the detour criterion compares the route's length with a free-space lower bound computed from the ports alone, so it needs no second search: the Manhattan distance between the source and target port centers plus the length two 90-degree bends take (`2 * bend_radius_um`), everything in micrometres. The route length is the bookkeeping's recorded `total_length_um` (what the GDS gets, after endpoint correction), not the router's cell set: `get_net_cells` returns the swept footprint with clearance, which is 1,500 to 1,650 cells for every 60 um net and would mark all 200 nets of the second Clements mesh (first run of the pass, see Surprises). A factor of 3 is the default when the pass is on; the spiral is a factor of 6.9 (477 um over a 69 um bound), the loop 4.9 (339 um), a normal S-bend about 0.8 (54.7 um) and a long mesh wire well under 2.
- 2026-10-06 (lead, deferred): the alternative of treating a detour as a failure inside the negotiation loop itself (so the net rips the blockers of its short corridor right away, `src/engine/negotiation/loop_.rs`) stays open as Milestone 4 if the two options do not resolve the private circuits; it changes the Rust engine and every attempt trace, and the owner wanted the two simpler measures tried first.


## Context and Orientation

The optical router lives in two halves. The Rust crate under `src/` does the searching: one grid A* per net over straight and 90-degree-bend primitives (`src/search/`), and a negotiation loop over many nets (`src/engine/negotiation/loop_.rs`, `run_negotiated_batch`) that routes nets in a given order, asks a probe who blocks a net that finds no path, rips those blockers up (the rip-up policy in `negotiation/ripup.rs`), searches again and re-queues the ripped nets for the next round. A net that finds any legal path is committed and never looked at again unless some later net rips it up. The Python side under `translation/routing/` prepares the jobs and drives the batch: `session.py` runs the nine phases in order (`stages.py` names them), `handoff.py` orders the jobs (`_topological_net_route_order`) and hands the static geometry to Rust, `dispatch.py` packs the jobs and calls `state.router.route_many_with_negotiated_repair_and_commit` (`dispatch_native_routing`), records every returned route (`_record_route`) and applies the endpoint corrections, and `verify_repair.py` runs the verifier and, for illegal crossings, a second negotiated batch on a selected subset of nets: it rips them (`state.router.ripup_route`, `state.route_bookkeeping.clear_route`), rebuilds their jobs with `_state_openings_for_job`, calls the same batch function and records the results. That subset batch is the template for the detour pass.

The order vocabulary is `translation/route_order.py`: `NET_ORDERS` lists the names, `order_route_jobs` sorts the jobs, and every existing rule starts with the source instance's depth in the net graph (`depth_by_node_from_jobs`: a coupler has depth 0, an MMI fed by a coupler depth 1, and so on) and ends with the declaration order (`route_index`). `topological-span` sorts by depth, then by the grid span `_route_job_grid_span` in `handoff.py` (the Manhattan distance of the two port states in cells), then by declaration. The CLI's `--net-order` takes its choices from `NET_ORDERS` (`python/photonic_router/cli.py`), the flow passes it through `python/photonic_router/flow.py` into `SessionSettings.net_order` (`translation/routing/settings.py`, normalized by `normalize_net_order`).

A route's length on the grid is the number of its cells (`route_cells_count` in the per-route diagnostics); its realized length in micrometres comes later from the centerline (`total_length_um` in the route records). The pass works in cells, before any realization, because both the route and the lower bound are grid quantities. The bend radius in cells is `state.bend_radius_cells`.

Every command below runs from the repository root `/home/benjamin/Documents/Repositories/working/TUMPhotonicRouter` with the project's virtual environment (`.venv/bin/python`). The Rust extension is already built (`maturin develop --release`); Milestones 1 and 2 touch only Python. The private circuits are routed with `python -m photonic_router route private.<name>`; their flags live in the generated modules under `benchmarks/private/` (git-ignored, written by `scripts/extract_private_benchmark.py`), and a measurement of the affected arm's feeds is the length of the route polygons in `build/routed_private.clements_2.gds` that touch the gap between `mmi2x2_34` and `waveguide_14`, exactly as the earlier analysis did: read the GDS with `klayout.db`, take the polygons on layer 1/0 that interact with the box x 20866..20870, y 13225..13250, and divide each polygon's area by the 0.5 um waveguide width. A clean S-bend measures 54.7 um; the spiral 457.5 um; the sibling's loop 329.1 um. Never pass `--debug-svgs` on these runs (it opens SVGs in the owner's browser); set `BROWSER=/bin/true` if a diagnostic run needs it.

## Milestone 2: the detour pass

Scope: after `dispatch_native_routing` has routed every net, an optional stage takes every net whose route length in cells exceeds `factor` times its free-space lower bound, rips all of them up at once, routes them again in one negotiated batch ordered by span, and records the new routes exactly as the first batch did; it repeats while the set of detoured nets shrinks (at most three passes) and prints one line per pass. After this milestone a user can run a benchmark with `--detour-pass-factor 3` and see, in the second Clements mesh, the two feeds of the affected arm re-routed to 54.7 um, with the stage reporting what it did.

Work. Create `translation/routing/detour_pass.py` with three functions. `free_lower_bound_cells(source_state, target_state, bend_radius_cells) -> int` returns `abs(dx) + abs(dy) + 2 * bend_radius_cells` from the two grid states (the states come from `_state_openings_for_job` in `dispatch.py`, the same ones the batch used). `select_detoured_net_ids(route_cells_by_net_id, bound_by_net_id, factor) -> list[int]` returns the net ids whose cell count exceeds `factor * bound`, sorted by bound ascending so that the batch routes the shortest first; it is pure and unit-tested. `run_detour_pass(settings, state, factor, max_passes=3) -> list[dict]` does the work: for every routed net (`state.route_bookkeeping.records_by_id` or the batch's route objects, whichever carries the cell count; `_record_route` in `dispatch.py` shows what a record holds), compute the bound from its job's states, select, and if the selection is empty return; otherwise rip the selected nets (`state.router.ripup_route(net_id)` and `state.route_bookkeeping.clear_route(net_id)`, the two calls `verify_repair.py` makes at its line 440 onwards), rebuild their batch tuples with `_state_openings_for_job`, `_clearance_exempt_cells_for_job`, `_foreign_keepout_cleanup_cells_for_job` and `_routing_endpoint_center_um` from `dispatch.py` (import them; they are module functions), call `state.router.route_many_with_negotiated_repair_and_commit` with the same radii and repair parameters `verify_repair.py` passes (`state.block_radius_cells`, `state.commit_radius_cells`, `state.core_commit_radius_cells`, `NEGOTIATED_MAX_ROUNDS`, the history weight and increment of `state.repair_config`), and if the batch status is not `routed`, restore nothing (the ripped nets are now unrouted; raise `RuntimeError` naming the pass and the failed net, because a detour pass must never silently lose a net) -- then record every returned route with `_record_route` and apply the checked endpoint corrections for those ids with `_apply_unrestricted_and_fanout_stub_endpoint_corrections_for_net_ids` as `verify_repair.py` does. Print, per pass, `- detour pass k: n nets over factor f (ids), re-routed, total cells before -> after`. Return the per-pass summaries for the stats. Repeat while the number of detoured nets decreased and `k < max_passes`.

Thread the option: a field `detour_pass_factor: float = 0.0` (0 means off) in the optical flow options next to `net_order` (`python/photonic_router/config.py`, the same dataclass `flow.py` reads `optical.net_order` from, lines near 385), a `--detour-pass-factor` flag in `python/photonic_router/cli.py` next to `--net-order`, the value into `SessionSettings` (`translation/routing/settings.py`, next to `net_order`), and the stage call in `translation/routing/session.py` right after `dispatch.dispatch_native_routing(settings, state, final.route_jobs)` (line 262) and before `verify_repair.repair_and_verify_final_geometry`, guarded by `if settings.detour_pass_factor > 0`. Add the stage to the phase list in `stages.py` as a `Protocol` like the others if the file declares one per phase (read `stages.py` first; if it only names the nine phases in a docstring, add the tenth there). Add the stats: `RoutingFlowStats` (`routing_flow_stats.py`) gets `detour_pass_reroutes: int | None` and the SUMMARY line a `detours=` field printing `-` when the pass did not run, following the pattern of the electrical counters there (they print `-` when the stage did not run).

Tests: `tests/test_detour_pass.py` with the pure functions (bounds for a few state pairs; selection with ties and factor edge; ordering by bound) and one test that runs `run_detour_pass` on a tiny synthetic state built the way `tests/test_route_order.py` or the dispatcher tests build settings and state, if such a fixture exists; otherwise the e2e test `tests/e2e/test_routing_flow_stats.py` gets a case running `heater_s_mod` with `--detour-pass-factor 3` and asserting the SUMMARY prints `detours=0` and the verifier is clean (the cascade has no detours, so the pass is a no-op and the result must equal the default run's route count).

Validation. `pytest tests/test_detour_pass.py tests/e2e/test_routing_flow_stats.py -q` green. `python -m photonic_router route private.clements_2 --detour-pass-factor 3` (with the module's own `--net-order topological-span`, i.e. without Milestone 1's order, to measure the pass alone): the stage line shows the two feeds (and any other detours) re-routed, the feeds measure 54.7 um, `verifier_errors=0`. Then the five private circuits with the pass; record the SUMMARY lines. Then both options together on all five.

## Milestone 4 (deferred, only if needed): the detour as a failure inside the loop

If the two options leave a detour on one of the private circuits that a cut-out of its surroundings routes cleanly, the criterion moves into `run_negotiated_batch` (`src/engine/negotiation/loop_.rs`): after `run_plain_attempt` returns `Routed`, compare the route's cell count with the free lower bound (the bound needs the job's source and target states, which `NativeRouteJob` carries) and, if it exceeds the factor, treat the outcome as `NotResolved` so that the probe names the blockers of the free corridor, the rip-up policy rips them, and the net searches again; accept the detour only after the ladder of attempts is exhausted, exactly as a failing net is accepted today. The factor would come from `NegotiationConfig` on both sides (`src/config.rs`, `python/photonic_router/config.py`) with an `ENV_OVERLAY` entry, and `negotiation/attempt.rs`'s trace line would carry the detour factor. This is a Rust change with its own tests (`loop_.rs` has a test per behaviour of the loop); do not start it without the owner's go.
